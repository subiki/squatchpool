Coin-Op Cutthroat

Solo with two Squatch Bots, two humans and a bot, or three local players. Protect your own color: blue, yellow or red. All 15 object balls are solid-colored; their numbers don't matter. Pocket any ball, even yours, to keep shooting. Scratch and the third player may steal one of your remaining balls, bringing an eliminated player back into the game. Last player with a ball wins. How to play · complete rules & controls

Three players defend three colors. Eliminate the other sets, but scratches can transfer a ball and revive a rival.

Players, colors and break

  1. Use fifteen object balls: five blue, five yellow and five red. The numbers printed on balls do not matter in this version.
  2. Player 1 breaks from the kitchen. Balls made on the break stay pocketed; the breaker may choose a color after a legal break pot.
  3. After your first legal pocket, including a legal break pot, choose an available color. You may wait until the end of that turn; after two players choose, the third gets the remaining color.

Turns and elimination

  1. On your turn, shoot at any object ball—an opponent's color or even your own. Each legally pocketed ball lets you keep shooting.
  2. A miss ends your turn. In the current implementation, failing to contact an object ball also ends the turn, without an extra ball-in-hand penalty.
  3. When your color has no balls remaining on the table, you are eliminated and skip turns unless a later scratch claim returns you to play.

The special scratch claim

  1. Pocketing the white cue ball is a scratch. The next active player receives ball-in-hand.
  2. The third player may choose one of the scratcher's remaining on-table balls and claim it as their own, or decline. The ball stays in the same place; only its ownership changes.
  3. The claimant can be a previously eliminated player. Taking ownership of that ball brings them back into the game. Balls already pocketed—even on fouls—are not re-spotted.

How to win

  1. The winner is the last player with at least one ball still belonging to them on the table. There is no special 8-ball finish and no called pocket requirement.
  2. Because you can pocket your own balls, clearing the wrong color may eliminate you. Think about who will remain alive after each shot.

Example: Blue scratches while two blue balls remain. Yellow gets ball-in-hand; Red (the third player) may claim one of Blue's on-table balls. Even if Red had already been eliminated, that claim puts Red back in the rack.

Playing on this site: Select 1, 2 or 3 local humans (bots fill empty seats). Break, aim from the white and shoot. The color picker appears above the table after your first legal pocket; scratch claims appear in More.

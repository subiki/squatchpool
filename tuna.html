🐟 Big Tuna Casserole

Two lowest-ball sequences: 1–10 and 11–15. Call your ball and pocket. The 10 wins once the 9 and 11–15 are gone—even if lower solids remain.

How Big Tuna Casserole works

Two independent ascending ball sequences, three scoring balls, and a 10-ball finish that rewards planning.

Complete rules · sequences, scoring, fouls & examples

Two independent ascending ball sequences, three scoring balls, and a 10-ball finish that rewards planning.

Rack and legal first contact

  1. Rack fifteen balls and break from the kitchen. The two sequences are 1–10 and 11–15; both are active at once.
  2. On every normal shot, your first contact must be the lowest numbered ball still on the table in either sequence. At the start, that means the 1 or the 11.
  3. As balls disappear, each sequence advances independently. For example, after the 1 and 11 are gone, the next legal first-contact targets are the 2 and 12.

Calling and pocketing

  1. Call the ball and pocket after the break. Your first-contact target must be legal, but the ball you call can be a different ball that you pocket through a legal combination.
  2. A correctly called pocketed ball stays down and you keep shooting. Balls pocketed without a matching legal call, or in an illegal shot, are spotted back onto the table.
  3. Contacting neither sequence's lowest available ball first—or scratching the white cue ball—is a foul. The incoming player receives ball-in-hand.

Money balls and the 10

  1. The 15 is worth 1 point, the 9 is worth 2, and the legally winning 10 is worth 3. These are match points, not real money.
  2. You may legally win with the 10 only after the 9 and all balls 11–15 are gone. Other lower solids (1–8) may still be on the table.
  3. If the 10 drops before you are eligible to win, it is spotted. Money-ball points accumulate across racks until you start a new match.

Rack and match flow

  1. The player who legally pockets the winning 10 takes the rack and breaks the next one.
  2. Use the displayed legal targets to choose your first contact. Plan ahead so you can clear the 9 and the high sequence while keeping a route to the 10.

Example: Suppose the 1 and 11 are down: you can first contact the 2 or 12. Once the 9 and 11–15 are gone, a legal called 10 wins even if the 3, 4 and 5 remain.
